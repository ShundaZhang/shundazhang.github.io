Discover a device and access its registers

PCIe devices expose configuration space. During enumeration the system discovers devices, sizes and assigns address windows described by BARs, and binds drivers. The driver maps a BAR and uses MMIO accessors such as readl/writel for register access. Register reads can have side effects, and posted writes may complete at the device after the CPU has continued. Follow the device and bus ordering rules.

x86 platforms can expose MMIO and a separate port-I/O space accessed by IN/OUT. RISC-V platforms commonly use MMIO for device registers. A driver also needs platform discovery, for example PCI configuration, ACPI tables or a device tree. QEMU virt is one documented RISC-V platform.

Walk through a DMA completion

A coherent DMA allocation keeps shared descriptor data coherent but still needs ordering before setting ownership bits. Streaming DMA mappings have a direction and ownership lifecycle. For a reused streaming mapping, perform the required synchronization when handing the buffer between CPU and device. Unmap only after the device has stopped using it.

On x86, common PCIe systems deliver interrupts via APIC infrastructure, with MSI/MSI-X represented as device writes. RISC-V boards may route interrupts through a PLIC or through AIA components such as APLIC and IMSIC. The OS manages masks, priorities and affinity. Polling trades CPU time for lower notification overhead at high completion rates.
